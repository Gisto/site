Privacy and developer control are Gisto's fundamental pillars. Gisto supports two authentication modes: GitHub/GitLab using access tokens, or local mode storing snippets in IndexedDB.

## How Gisto secures your data

When you connect cloud sync providers like GitHub or GitLab, Gisto interfaces directly with their official public APIs from your desktop:

- **No intermediary servers:** Gisto has no backend servers that process, inspect, or backup your snippets or access tokens.
- **Token storage:** Gisto only saves the Access token in your local storage and nothing else. This token will be saved permanently until you log out, or it will expire.
- **Direct sync:** Synchronization requests go directly from your local Gisto client to GitHub or GitLab servers.

## Local Mode

No account required. Your snippets are stored in your browser's local database using IndexedDB. You can export and import your snippets as JSON files from Settings.

## Why isn't the app signed?

As an open-source, non-profit community project, Gisto binaries are not code-signed. As a result, your operating system may trigger a warning dialog during installation:

- **Windows SmartScreen warning:** Alerts that the publisher is "Unknown". Click **More Info** and select **Run anyway**.
- **macOS Gatekeeper warning:** Alerts that the developer cannot be verified. Go to **System Preferences > Security & Privacy > General** and click **Open Anyway**.

Because Gisto is 100% open source, the entire codebase is fully auditable on GitHub.
